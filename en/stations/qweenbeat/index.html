Qween Beat

L7 · Ballroom & queer

MikeQ dates Qween Beat back to 2005, first as the name he released his music under, then as a collective; the label put out its first official release, Qweendom, in August 2016. Their ball music builds on the "ha" and the crash of "The Ha Dance"; it was sampled by Beyoncé in 2022.

Heir to the drag balls documented as early as the 1890s, contemporary ballroom culture emerged in response to racial discrimination in drag pageants and took shape in New York around the houses in the early 1970s. In 1967, Crystal LaBeija walked off the stage of a pageant at New York's Town Hall to denounce its racial standards; in 1972, she and Lottie LaBeija threw the first annual House of LaBeija Ball. In 1991, Masters At Work's The Ha Dance provided a sonic template that ballroom artists made their own. During the 2000s, Vjuan Allure transformed this material with Allure Ha. MikeQ, Qween Beat and the generations that followed have extended and renewed this musical language. This lineage concerns the evolution of ballroom music, not the origin of the community that makes it.
