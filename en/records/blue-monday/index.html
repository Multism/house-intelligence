Why this record

The bridge between Manchester post-punk and house, a year before Chicago's first house 12".

How it was made

An Oberheim DMX drum machine programmed by hand, a long sequenced bassline, and a rock band choosing to be replaced by machines. The record owes a lot to Kraftwerk, Donna Summer and the New York electro-disco the band heard in Manhattan.

Origins and production approach

New Order rose from the ashes of Joy Division after Ian Curtis's death in 1980. Peter Hook, Bernard Sumner, Stephen Morris and Gillian Gilbert moved from post-punk to dance music after discovering New York's clubs, and produced the track themselves.

Club sociology

Factory funded the Haçienda, which opened in May 1982 and lost money for years. "Blue Monday" is described as the best-selling 12" of all time. According to Tony Wilson its die-cut floppy-disk sleeve lost 5 pence on every copy, a legend Peter Saville says he is tired of.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The track draws on electro-disco and hi-NRG from New York gay clubs (Sylvester, Donna Summer), yet New Order rarely frames that as a debt. An angle to document rather than assert.
