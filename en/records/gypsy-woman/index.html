Why this record

A dancefloor hit that speaks about the street.

How it was made

A keyboard riff, a heavy bassline and the famous scatted "la da dee, la da da". It is a club hit with a social lyric: the song portrays a homeless woman who wears make-up and sees herself as beautiful while busking.

Clavier (riff)Boîte à rythmes

Origins and production approach

Crystal Waters had written poetry since childhood and worked for the District of Columbia government, in the computer division issuing arrest warrants. She wrote the lyric with Neal Conway, inspired by a woman she saw outside the Mayflower Hotel. Baltimore's Basement Boys produced the record.

Club sociology

Number 2 in the UK, number 8 in the US and number one on US club charts and in several European countries: a worldwide hit. It shows that early-'90s American house could tell of urban poverty over a euphoric groove.

Ballroom & queer scenes

Editorial reading, to be sourced: the "la da dee" circulates in ballroom remixes and on vogue floors.
