Why this record

House turned lullaby.

How it was made

A simple piano melody, an arpeggiated bass and a steady kick: "dream house", melodic and gentle trance.

PianoSynthétiseurs

Origins and production approach

Roberto Concina, born in Switzerland and based in Fagagna (Friuli), gave two reasons for the track: photographs of children who were victims of the Bosnian war, and the wish for a calm end-of-night record to reduce road deaths among young Italians leaving clubs.

Club sociology

Released in Italy in January 1995 on DBX, it sold little before being licensed by Platipus, then Deconstruction. It went on to become a worldwide hit (number 1 on Billboard's dance chart in 1996): trance turned into European pop.

Ballroom & queer scenes

With no queer axis of its own, it is a collective, apolitical reverie.

Reference

Hypnotised: A Journey Through Trance Music (1990–2005) — Arjan Rietveld (2022). Dream house and melodic trance turned European pop.
