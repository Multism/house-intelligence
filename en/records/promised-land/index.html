Why this record

It shows house has always carried a message of emancipation.

How it was made

Luminous piano chords, a gospel progression and a lyric of hope ("brothers, sisters, one day we will be free"). House as a civil-rights hymn.

Boîte à rythmesPiano & orgue

Origins and production approach

Joe Smooth, a Chicago DJ and producer schooled in soul and gospel, wrote a political anthem in a genre reputed to be apolitical.

Club sociology

Released in the US in 1987, the record only came out officially in the UK in February 1989, the same day The Style Council released their cover. Its circulation on import during summer 1988, at the parties Wayne Anthony describes in Class of '88, still needs confirming in the book.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The "promised land" of a Black and queer collective became the utopia of white British youth, a transfer of meaning at the heart of house history.

Reference

Energy Never Dies: Afro-Optimism and Creativity in Chicago — Ayana Contreras (2021). Chicago Afro-optimism: house as a hymn of Black hope.
