Why this record

The future of house and techno is already audible in 1983.

How it was made

An icy synth line, an 808 electro rhythm and futurist stabs: a bridge between Kraftwerk and Detroit funk.

Roland TR-808 Rhythm ComposerSynthétiseurs

Origins and production approach

Juan Atkins, one of the Belleville Three, and Rick Davis, a Vietnam veteran obsessed with futurism. Atkins lays the foundations of what would become techno.

Club sociology

In Detroit, middle-class Black youth heard Kraftwerk on the radio (The Electrifying Mojo). Dan Sicko shows how this futurism grew out of a city in industrial decline.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The techno narrative pays little attention to queer scenes. Sicko nonetheless notes the influence of Detroit's gay-club DJs on the Belleville generation.

Reference

Techno Rebels: The Renegades of Electronic Funk — Dan Sicko (1999). Juan Atkins, Cybotron and Detroit electro.
