Why this record

All of house descends from this break: the instrumental loop as a site of collective trance.

How it was made

A Philadelphia studio orchestra: silky strings, horns and a bass-and-drums groove that stands on its own. The long instrumental break became DJs' raw material, looped on two decks for minutes on end.

Section rythmique liveCordes & cuivres (orchestre)

Origins and production approach

MFSB ("Mother Father Sister Brother") gathered Gamble & Huff's session players, the hit machine of Philadelphia soul: studio pros, not club musicians.

Club sociology

An anthem of David Mancuso's Loft and New York's first underground discotheques. Vince Aletti tracked it week after week in his Record World column, logging what DJs played before the industry noticed.

Ballroom & queer scenes

One of the founding records of 1970s New York's Black and Latino gay dancefloor. Its message of universal love literally becomes the dancefloor's promise.

Reference

Love Saves the Day: A History of American Dance Music Culture, 1970–1979 — Tim Lawrence (2003). David Mancuso's Loft and 1970s New York DJ culture.
