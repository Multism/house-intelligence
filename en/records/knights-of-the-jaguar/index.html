Why this record

Techno as a political act.

How it was made

Epic string pads, a relentless 909 and a melodic theme that rises without end: Detroit at its most lyrical.

Roland TR-909 Rhythm ComposerSynthétiseur (cordes)

Origins and production approach

Rolando Rocha, a Chicano DJ from Detroit, joined Underground Resistance, Mike Banks and Jeff Mills's militant collective that rejected the industry and its faces.

Club sociology

Sony tried to license it, then released a cover ("Jaguar", 2000). UR answered with a cease-and-desist and called on record shops and DJs to boycott. The details remain disputed, but the affair made the track a symbol of resistance against the commodification of techno.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The title evokes Aztec warriors: a Chicano imagery of resistance, with no queer anchoring.

Reference

Techno Rebels: The Renegades of Electronic Funk — Dan Sicko (1999). Underground Resistance and Detroit's militant ethic; the Sony affair as reported in the press (Hot Press).
