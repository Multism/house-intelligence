Why this record

At 113 BPM you instantly feel that house can slow down without losing its power to move a floor.

How it was made

The log drum, a percussive bass with a signature glide, carries the melody. Constant shakers, a discreet kick and a slow tempo (around 113 BPM), with Focalistic's Sepitori flow floating on top. The groove is horizontal: it sways more than it pushes.

Origins and production approach

Vigro Deep, a teenage producer from Mabopane, belongs to the generation that shaped amapiano on FL Studio in Gauteng's townships. Focalistic raps in Pretoria slang.

Club sociology

Amapiano emerged in the mid-2010s at neighbourhood parties, in taxis and taverns across Gauteng. The 2020 lockdown propelled it through social media and livestreams; the Davido remix opened the pan-African market.

Ballroom & queer scenes

Johannesburg's queer scenes have embraced amapiano. Carried by the townships, the genre reopens the question of who holds the keys to house: Black youth, this time in Africa.
