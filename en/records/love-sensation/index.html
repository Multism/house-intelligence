Why this record

The direct bridge between Salsoul disco and sample-based Italo house.

How it was made

A disco production by Dan Hartman, who wrote and produced the track, built around a monumental gospel voice. According to The Independent, Loleatta Holloway is the most sampled female singer in popular music.

Orchestre discoSection rythmique live

Origins and production approach

Born in Chicago in 1946, Holloway sang gospel with her mother in the Holloway Community Singers before recording for Gold Mind, a subsidiary of Salsoul, the New York label whose 12" mixes invented the club remix.

Club sociology

In 1989 Black Box sampled her voice without permission or credit for "Ride on Time", six weeks at number one and the UK's best-selling single of the year. Holloway and Hartman sued the group in 1990 and reached an out-of-court settlement; Holloway was later credited as featured vocalist. The case became a textbook example of sampling rights and of how Black women singers were treated.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The gospel diva, a central figure of the dancefloor's queer imagination. Her voice, chopped up by others, also tells of the erasure of Black women in dance music.
