Why this record

It is the record most often cited as the first house 12" ever pressed, a debated attribution: there are precedents, and RA calls the A-side itself "debatable". It matters less for its music than for the gesture it embodies: anyone with an 808 could now make a record.

How it was made

A looped TR-808, a bouncing synth bass and a vocal reduced to a mantra. Saunders recreated a disco edit he played in clubs, Mach's "On & On", which had been stolen from him: house begins as a homemade copy of a vanished record.

Origins and production approach

A resident DJ at the Playground in the early 1980s, Saunders recorded the track in 1983 with Vince Lawrence and released it in early 1984 on their own label, Jes Say Records. It was pressed by Larry Sherman, who then ran the city's only pressing plant and would go on to found Trax Records. The model was set: a DJ, some machines, an independent pressing and a direct route to local record bins.

Club sociology

Chicago was coming out of the 1979 "Disco Demolition Night", when rock radio declared disco dead. Yet disco survived in South Side clubs and on WBMX, where the Hot Mix 5 broadcast mixes that teenagers taped off the radio. The self-released 12" answered a shortage: there simply wasn't enough new disco left to feed the dancefloors.

Ballroom & queer scenes

According to the most common version, the word "house" comes from the Warehouse, a club whose crowd was mostly Black gay men and where Frankie Knuckles played from 1977 to 1982 (the etymology is disputed). Saunders belonged to a younger, more mixed scene: the sound left its queer cradle very early and spread across the city.
