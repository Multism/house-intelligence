Why this record

Proof that house changes as it travels: slowed down, saturated, pop.

How it was made

A distorted, filtered synth-bass riff halfway between funk and acid, over a compressed 909. The slow tempo and distortion give a heavy, almost hip-hop groove, closer to G-funk than to Chicago.

Origins and production approach

Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo came from indie rock (Darlin'). They discovered house through raves and Chicago records, which they name-check in "Teachers". A scholarly fans' approach, not that of club DJs.

Club sociology

The record first came out on Soma, Slam's Glasgow label, before Virgin signed the duo. The French Touch was built on export: it was recognised in London before Paris.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The Paris scene crystallised at the Respect nights at Queen, a gay club on the Champs-Élysées. Their tour was called "Respect Is Burning", a direct nod to Paris Is Burning. Queer codes were cited as cultural reference more than lived.
