Why this record

The 303 moving from club to rave.

How it was made

A screaming 303, a dry kick and a robotic voice. British acid gets faster and more aggressive, with one foot already in hardcore.

Roland TB-303 Bass LineVoix synthétique

Origins and production approach

Brian Dougans, later of Future Sound of London, met the Stakker video collective in Manchester as they looked for music for their computer graphics. Morgan Khan (Street Sounds) sent him to record at Ealing's Dance Studios with engineer John Laker; the track was written in two days.

Club sociology

It reached the UK top 20 in 1988, at the height of the acid house moral panic. The flyers and membership cards of the period, archived in Rave Art, show the smiley, futurist aesthetic of those nights.

Ballroom & queer scenes

No explicit queer anchoring: this is rave acid, that of British youth reclaiming the Chicago sound.

Reference

Rave Art: Flyers, Invitations and Membership Cards from the Birth of Acid House Clubs and Raves — Chelsea Louise Berlin (2009). The iconography of 1988–1989 flyers and membership cards.
