Why this record

The birth certificate of the club remix on a 12-inch.

How it was made

Walter Gibbons did not just remix, he re-cut: isolating percussion and breaks on tape, he turned the album's 6:51 into a mix of nearly ten minutes. Released in May 1976, it was the first 12" sold commercially (12" promos had circulated in clubs since 1975).

Origins and production approach

Double Exposure was a Philadelphia vocal group. Gibbons, a New York DJ, built his name through club mixing before Salsoul hired him.

Club sociology

This is when Vince Aletti was reporting in Record World what New York DJs were playing. The 12" came out of DJ demand: more volume, more length, more bass in the groove.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The audience of the clubs where Gibbons played this record was largely Black, Latino and gay; DJs were the first tastemakers, ahead of radio.
