Why this record

The first great acid classic made outside the United States, proof that house had become a language others could speak.

How it was made

A more melodic 303 than Phuture's, a light 808 groove and a sampled voice chopped into abstraction. The track was re-recorded in two days at Manchester's Moonraker Studios in June 1988.

Origins and production approach

Gerald Simpson, a young Black Mancunian who passed through 808 State, produced it in his bedroom on second-hand gear. His approach is British: he absorbed Chicago house through imports and tapes, then mixed it with dub and electro.

Club sociology

An anthem of 1988's Second Summer of Love: the Haçienda, warehouse parties, then the orbital raves around the M25. British club culture became a mass movement and a political problem that led to the 1994 Criminal Justice Act.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The Haçienda would launch Flesh (1991–1995), its big gay night, a reminder that Manchester's acid scene wasn't only laddish. Garratt documents the uneasy coexistence of London's gay clubs and straight raves.

Reference

Adventures in Wonderland: Acid House, Rave and the UK Club Explosion — Sheryl Garratt (1998). Account of the Second Summer of Love and the Haçienda.
