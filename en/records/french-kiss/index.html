Why this record

Start the Pulse: the record's real tempo is itself a BPM manipulation.

How it was made

A haunting synth riff, a moaning female voice and, above all, a tempo that slows almost to a halt before starting up again. One of the rare club hits built on a BPM slowdown.

Boîte à rythmesSynthétiseur

Origins and production approach

Marvin Louis Burns, son of a blues musician, was a DJ who threw big parties in Chicago. He built the track as a dramatic build designed for the floor.

Club sociology

Banned by many radio stations for its moans, it became a European hit. It epitomises the club as a space of open eroticism.

Ballroom & queer scenes

Its explicit eroticism extends the legacy of Chicago's gay dancefloors, where dancing and sex were never separate.

Reference

Last Night a DJ Saved My Life — Bill Brewster & Frank Broughton (1999). Late-80s Chicago: house goes international.
