Why this record

Deep house told by the people it forgot.

How it was made

Ten tracks, nearly eighty minutes of slow deep house, opened by a voiceover: "House is not universal. House is hyper-specific." The titles spell out the programme: "Ball'r (Madonna-Free Zone)", "House Music Is Controllable Desire You Can Own".

SamplerVoix

Origins and production approach

DJ Sprinkles is one of Terre Thaemlitz's names. Born in Minnesota, Terre Thaemlitz moved to New York in 1986 to study at the Cooper Union, then to Japan (Kawasaki) in 2001. It is the first album under this name; it was reissued in 2014 by Thaemlitz's label Comatonse.

Club sociology

The record is a critique of deep house turned commodity. It recalls where it came from: sexual and gender crises, trans sex work, black-market hormones, HIV, ACT UP, Tompkins Square Park, queer-bashing. Resident Advisor named it its number one album of 2009.

Ballroom & queer scenes

Central: "Ball'r (Madonna-Free Zone)" ends with a text criticising Madonna's "Vogue" for erasing the queer, Black and Latino origins of voguing and the balls.
