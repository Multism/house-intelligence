Why this record

The direct bridge between New York house and ballroom: an instrumental that became collective choreography.

How it was made

A "ha!" sampled from the train scene of the film Trading Places (1983), a crash cymbal and a nervy rhythm. The beat works as a movement score: every "ha" calls for a pose, every crash for a dip.

E-mu SP-1200Akai MPC60 MIDI Production Center

Origins and production approach

Louie Vega (Bronx, from a Puerto Rican salsa family) and Kenny "Dope" Gonzalez (Brooklyn, hip-hop beatmaker). Their partnership fused hip-hop sampler culture with New York's Latin and garage heritage.

Club sociology

Born in the early-90s ecosystem of New York clubs and radio, just as house was professionalising around star remixers.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The ballroom track par excellence: the crash on the fourth beat became the cue for vogue femme's dip. The "ha" comes from a Trading Places scene that includes blackface and slurs: Black and Latina, queer and trans ballroom turned it around, reissuing and remixing it endlessly for more than thirty years.

Reference

Paris Is Burning — Jennie Livingston (1990). The ballroom context (houses, categories, vogue) this beat would come to structure.
