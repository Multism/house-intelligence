Why this record

The direct link between disco and machine house.

How it was made

Everything is synthetic except the kick and the vocal: an arpeggiated bass sequenced on a Moog modular, pulsing sixteenth notes, Summer's voice floating weightless. For the first time a machine carries an entire disco song.

Moog modulaireSéquenceur analogique

Origins and production approach

Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte worked out of Munich, far from American soul. They imagined a record "from the future" to close a concept album travelling through the decades.

Club sociology

The record links orchestral disco and electronic dance music. Brian Eno reportedly heard in it "the sound of the future" of clubbing.

Ballroom & queer scenes

Donna Summer was the absolute icon of late-70s gay dancefloors. "I Feel Love" paved the way for hi-NRG, the electronic disco of 1980s gay clubs, and, via Patrick Cowley, for a whole strand of queer music.

Reference

Hot Stuff: Disco and the Remaking of American Culture — Alice Echols (2010). Disco, technology and American gay culture.
