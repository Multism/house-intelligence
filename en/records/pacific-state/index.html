Why this record

The soundtrack of Manchester in 1989.

How it was made

Birdsong, a supple 808 rhythm, a discreet acid bass and a jazz saxophone: balearic acid, made for dawn at the Haçienda.

Roland TR-808 Rhythm ComposerRoland TB-303 Bass LineSaxophone

Origins and production approach

Graham Massey, Martin Price and Gerald Simpson (before he left) met around Manchester's Eastern Bloc record shop: a collective of record fans turned producers.

Club sociology

The original version came out on the mini-album Quadrastate (Creed) in July 1989, before the ZTT single in November (UK number 10). It was an end-of-night anthem at the Haçienda's Hot nights. Gerald Simpson, who had left by then, disputed the credits. Peter Hook also tells the flip side of the era: gang violence, drugs and the bankruptcy of a beloved but unmanageable club.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The Haçienda would host Flesh, its gay night, but 808 State stayed rooted in a mostly male, straight Mancunian culture, that of "Madchester".

Reference

The Haçienda: How Not to Run a Club — Peter Hook (2009). The Haçienda seen from inside by one of its owners.
