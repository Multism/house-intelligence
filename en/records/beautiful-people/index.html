Why this record

It lets you hear 1990s New York before the clubs closed.

How it was made

A nervy garage groove, organ riffs and Barbara Tucker's gospel voice calling the floor: 1990s New York garage at its most festive.

OrgueBoîte à rythmes

Origins and production approach

Produced by Little Louie Vega (Masters At Work) for Strictly Rhythm, the New York label that dominated American house that decade. Tucker was also a dancer and party promoter.

Club sociology

The golden age of New York garage (Sound Factory, Sound Factory Bar, Shelter), before the Giuliani-era closures that DJ Disciple describes as the scene's "fall".

Ballroom & queer scenes

Title and chorus celebrate the collective beauty of the dancefloor: an anthem of Manhattan's gay and mixed clubs, carried by a gospel diva.

Reference

The Beat, The Scene, The Sound: A DJ's Journey Through the Rise, Fall, and Rebirth of House Music in New York City — DJ Disciple & Henry Kronk (2023). 1990s New York house, from the inside.
