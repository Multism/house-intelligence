Why this record

The loop closes: house returns to the top of the charts by claiming its Black and queer lineage.

How it was made

A sample of Robin S's "Show Me Love" (and its M1 organ) and an interpolation of "Explode" by New Orleans bounce artist Big Freedia. The hook is built for the floor; production by Beyoncé, The-Dream and Tricky Stewart.

Samples : « Show Me Love » (Robin S), « Explode » (Big Freedia)

Origins and production approach

On Renaissance Beyoncé claims a lineage of Black club music, with an explicit tribute to house pioneers.

Club sociology

The track went to number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and the US dance chart, reopening the question of house's visibility beyond its original scenes.

Ballroom & queer scenes

The record directly references ballroom and Black queer house, as a mainstream tribute: read alongside Vogue (1990) and Show Me Love.

Reference

Do You Remember House? Chicago's Queer of Color Undergrounds — Micah E. Salkind (2019). Queer genealogy of house.
