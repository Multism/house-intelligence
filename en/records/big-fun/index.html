Why this record

It shows Detroit also made pop, and that British export shaped the history of these genres.

How it was made

A joyful synth bass, chordal pads and the voice of Paris Grey, a Chicago singer. Saunderson builds a bright, pop techno designed for collective euphoria.

Roland TR-909 Rhythm ComposerYamaha DX100

Origins and production approach

Kevin Saunderson, a former Belleville athlete, had the most commercial instincts of the three. The duo with Paris Grey formally united Detroit and Chicago.

Club sociology

The single made the UK top 10 and turned Detroit into a brand in Europe. Britain became the first big market for music born in Black American cities that remained marginal at home.

Ballroom & queer scenes

Paris Grey's voice extends the tradition of the club diva, a central figure of house's queer imagination, at the heart of a "techno" project.

Reference

Join the Future: Bleep Techno and the Birth of British Bass Music — Matt Anniss (2019). Britain's reception of Detroit and the birth of bleep/bass music.
